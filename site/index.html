Investigating clustering robustness under systematic missingness

A computational investigation into how missing-data mechanisms, preprocessing assumptions, and model uncertainty influence the reliability of unsupervised learning.

On this page

Research abstract

When the data get larger, what stays distorted?

Missing sales records and genuine zero sales have different meanings. In a segmentation pipeline, the rule used to fill those records can change the structure the algorithm discovers.

All stores and sales are synthetic. The study estimates behavior under the specified simulation, and its business implications are illustrative.

Research questions

Five questions about robustness

  1. Missingness and preprocessing sensitivity

    How do mechanism, severity and imputation jointly affect benchmark agreement?

  2. Validation metric reliability

    Can a stronger silhouette conceal substantial changes to individual assignments?

  3. Data volume and systematic distortion

    How does adding training stores change agreement when missingness persists?

  4. Experimental variability

    Do average rankings hold across new synthetic cohorts and masking draws?

  5. Confidence and assignment stability

    Does a concentrated model posterior coincide with a stable K-means assignment?

Methodology

From complete sales to cluster validation

  1. Synthetic stores
  2. Complete reference
  3. Mask product cells
  4. Impute
  5. Fit K-means
  6. Evaluate
  7. Aggregate by seed

Four overlapping profiles generate traffic, basket value, daypart proportions and correlated product sales. Traffic times basket value gives total sales. A category-specific hurdle produces structural zeros that can be observed or masked. Profile labels are held outside the feature matrix.

MCAR
Each eligible cell is independently masked with constant probability r. Its observed and underlying values have no role.
MAR
The mask depends on fully observed standardized log traffic and evening share. The logit is b − z(log(1 + traffic)) + 0.55 z(evening) + product offset.
MNAR
The logit is b − 1.25 z(log(1 + product sales)), standardized within product. This depends on the value that the mask can hide. For MAR and MNAR, b sets the expected product-cell masking rate.

Zero fill treats missing sales as absent sales. Median fill uses the observed marginal center. KNN uses five distance-weighted neighbors in observed-data standardized features. Iterative fill uses chained Bayesian ridge models (up to 15 iterations). Multivariate estimates return to original sales units and are clipped at zero; original observed cells remain intact. These methods depend on their modeling assumptions under MNAR.

Each dataset is standardized feature by feature before fitting K-means with four clusters and 12 initializations. The algorithm minimizes the within-cluster sum of squared Euclidean distances.

The complete-data model uses the same recipe and defines the comparison partition. Its labels need not match the overlapping latent profiles. In the original first-seed check over two to eight clusters, the elbow rule chose four and silhouette chose two across the complete and examined masked conditions.

How to read the evaluation metrics

ARI compares partitions with correction for chance. One corresponds to identical partitions up to label permutation. Values near zero indicate agreement near the chance reference. Silhouette compares average within-cluster distance with the nearest other cluster's average distance. It uses a fixed seeded subset of up to 1,000 stores per cohort. Assignment change rate is the fraction that changes clusters after optimal Hungarian alignment.

GMM confidence is the highest estimated posterior across mixture components on a single iterative-imputed frame. K-means stability is the most frequent aligned cluster share across 48 masking and imputation scenarios in the original uncertainty cohort. These measures use different models and answer different questions.

Research results

Evidence across the experiments

01 Imputation can change the segmentation

Within each seed, all methods see the same 5,000-store dataset and the same mask at a given mechanism and target. The heatmap compares outcomes to the complete-data reference.

Figure 4. Means across usable seeds. Each cell links to its confidence interval, spread and seed-level values in the lab.

Downstream decisions should examine changes in partition membership as well as reconstruction error. These ranks are conditional on the selected imputation implementations and sales generator.

02 Stronger separation can coexist with assignment changes

For each run we subtract the silhouette of its seed-matched complete-data partition and measure the share of assignments changed after alignment.

Figure 5. The vertical guide marks zero silhouette gain. Purple points mark improved silhouette with at least 10% of stores reassigned, a descriptive threshold.

Silhouette describes geometry after preprocessing and does not test membership consistency. The thresholds are descriptive and paired configurations share seeds.

03 Larger training cohorts can leave residual disagreement

In the separate original sample-size study, clean and imputed models were trained on nested samples and evaluated on the same independent 2,500-store holdout within each seed. The clean reference used the largest training cohort and complete evaluation features.

Figure 6. Seed outcomes, mean lines and 95% seed-bootstrap mean intervals. The finite experiment combines training and evaluation missingness and gives no asymptotic estimate.

04 Mean ranks conceal variation across seeds

Within each design cell the paired ARI difference measures the effect in ARI units after matching on seed and mask. Rank shares count how often each method has the highest ARI, splitting credit for ties.

Figure 7. Paired means with 95% seed-bootstrap intervals. The lower tail and winner share use empirical outcomes.

Ranking stability helps decide whether a pipeline needs contingency checks. Intervals are marginal descriptions of the simulated design; no multiplicity-adjusted significance claims are made.

Observation-level uncertainty

05 Confidence and stability can disagree

The original 5,000-store cohort provides a one-cohort diagnostic. A GMM fits the 30% MNAR iterative frame. Repeated K-means fits under 48 alternative scenarios track aligned membership frequencies.

Figure 8. Purple identifies stores with maximum posterior ≥ 0.80 and stability < 0.60. The thresholds illustrate a review policy, and the GMM posteriors have not been calibrated against assignment correctness.

Discussion

How to use these diagnostics

In a retail workflow, audit cases where a strong internal score coexists with changed membership. Check whether incomplete records reflect category absence or a reporting problem before using cluster assignments for promotions or assortment planning. A manager might prioritize review where labels move under plausible changes or where the posterior and stability signals disagree.

Limitations and future research

The synthetic generator, simplified masking functions, scaling, Euclidean geometry and K-means reference limit external validity. The study does not estimate business outcomes. MNAR assumptions cannot generally be identified from observed data alone. An extreme or alternative synthetic generator, public transaction data, stochastic multiple imputation, Bayesian mixtures and decision-oriented validation would test how far the results extend.

Seed-to-seed variability concerns repeated simulation. Systematic distortion concerns disagreement relative to a reference. Posterior confidence is conditional on a fitted model, scenario stability depends on selected assumptions, and uncertainty about the data-generating process lies outside the reported seed intervals.

Literature and foundations

Rubin formalizes missing-data assumptions, and Little and Rubin connect them to likelihood and imputation. Rousseeuw motivates the internal silhouette diagnostic. Von Luxburg surveys the rationale and caveats of clustering stability. Meng's account of data quality and quantity motivates our finite-sample comparison without providing a theoretical result for K-means.
